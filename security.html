// Frameworks We Audit Against

COMPLIANCE REFERENCE

Frameworks assessed in every AI Stack Doctor governance audit. Updated July 2026.
EU AI Act (2024)
European Union
Critical
Effective: Aug 1 2024 (prohibited practices Feb 2 2025; GPAI Aug 2 2025; high-risk Aug 2 2026; regulated-product AI Aug 2 2028)
  • Prohibited AI practices banned from Feb 2025 (social scoring, real-time biometric surveillance in public)
  • High-risk AI systems require conformity assessment, registration, human oversight
  • General Purpose AI (GPAI) models >10^25 FLOPs face systemic risk obligations
Consequence: Up to €35M or 7% global annual turnover
Official reference →
GDPR (2018) + AI Implications
European Union
Critical
Effective: May 2018 (ongoing enforcement)
  • Lawful basis required for training AI on personal data
  • Data minimisation — only collect what AI genuinely needs
  • Right to erasure ('right to be forgotten') — must be honoured in AI systems
Consequence: Up to €20M or 4% global annual turnover
Official reference →
EU Data Act (2024)
European Union
High
Effective: Sep 2025
  • Data sharing obligations between businesses and consumers
  • Cloud switching rights — must support data portability
  • B2G data sharing in public emergencies
Consequence: Up to €20M or 4% global turnover
Official reference →
EU Digital Services Act / DSA (2022)
European Union
High
Effective: Feb 2024 (VLOPs/VLOSEs from Aug 2023)
  • Algorithmic transparency and explainability for recommender systems
  • Annual risk assessments for Very Large Online Platforms (VLOPs)
  • Ad targeting restrictions (profiling minors prohibited)
Consequence: Up to 6% global annual turnover; repeat offenders can be banned
Official reference →
US Executive Order on AI (Oct 2023)
United States
Critical
Effective: Oct 2023 (ongoing agency rulemaking)
  • AI safety testing for foundation models before deployment (NIST AI RMF alignment)
  • Mandatory reporting for AI models trained above compute thresholds
  • Watermarking/authentication of AI-generated content
Consequence: Contractual and procurement consequences for federal contractors
Official reference →
CCPA / CPRA (2020/2023)
United States — California
Critical
Effective: Jan 2020 / Jan 2023
  • Right to know what personal data is collected and used in AI training
  • Right to opt-out of sale or sharing of personal data
  • Right to correct inaccurate personal data
Consequence: Up to $7,500 per intentional violation
Official reference →
NIST AI Risk Management Framework (2023)
United States (voluntary, widely adopted globally)
High
Effective: Jan 2023
  • GOVERN: Establish AI risk culture, policies, and accountability structures
  • MAP: Identify and categorise AI risks in context
  • MEASURE: Analyse and assess AI risks with metrics
Consequence: Voluntary — but referenced in US federal procurement and sector regulation
Official reference →
UK AI Regulation (Pro-Innovation Approach)
United Kingdom
High
Effective: 2024 onwards (existing law + sector guidance)
  • Principles-based approach: safety, transparency, fairness, accountability, contestability
  • Sector-specific regulators enforce AI rules (FCA, ICO, CMA, MHRA)
  • ICO Guidance on AI and data protection (GDPR UK equivalent)
Consequence: Sector-specific — ICO up to £17.5M or 4% global turnover
Official reference →
China Generative AI Regulations (2023)
China
Critical
Effective: Aug 2023
  • Content must adhere to socialist core values; no content subverting state power
  • Training data must have clear IP licensing
  • Mandatory labelling of AI-generated content
Consequence: Service suspension, fines, criminal liability for severe cases
Official reference →
ISO/IEC 42001 — AI Management System (2023)
Global (ISO standard)
High
Effective: Dec 2023
  • Establish an AI Management System (AIMS) with documented policies
  • AI risk assessment and impact assessment procedures
  • Roles and responsibilities for AI governance
Consequence: Voluntary — but increasingly required by enterprise procurement
Official reference →
HIPAA + AI (US Healthcare)
United States — Healthcare
Critical
Effective: Ongoing; HHS AI guidance 2024
  • PHI cannot be used to train AI models without explicit authorisation or de-identification
  • Business Associate Agreements (BAA) required with AI vendors
  • AI clinical decision support tools may require FDA clearance
Consequence: Up to $1.9M per violation category per year
Official reference →
PCI-DSS v4.0 + AI (Payment)
Global — Payment Industry
High
Effective: Mar 2024 (v4.0)
  • AI fraud detection systems must not store raw PAN data beyond need
  • Penetration testing must include AI/ML components
  • Automated security testing of AI pipelines handling card data
Consequence: Fines from card brands; loss of ability to process payments
Official reference →
SOC 2 Type II + AI Controls
Global (AICPA standard, widely required)
High
Effective: Ongoing — annual audits
  • AI model access controls and logical security
  • Change management for model updates and retraining
  • Availability SLAs for AI inference services
Consequence: Loss of enterprise customers; failed procurement requirements
Official reference →
CMMC 2.0
United States — Department of Defense
Critical
Effective: 2021 (v2.0 finalized November 2021)
  • Required for any organization in the DoD supply chain. Level 2 requires third-party assessment. Level 3 targets advanced persistent threats.
  • AI systems processing CUI must meet CMMC controls. Audit logging, access control, and incident response requirements apply to AI pipelines.
FedRAMP 20x
United States — Federal Government
Critical
Effective: 2024 (20x modernization pathway active)
  • Required for any SaaS/cloud tool used by federal agencies. 20x pathway uses machine-readable packages and continuous monitoring for faster ATO.
  • AI tools deployed in federal environments must achieve FedRAMP authorization. Continuous monitoring and incident reporting obligations apply.
Texas TRAIGA
United States — Texas
High
Effective: January 1 2026
  • Imposes categorical restrictions on AI for behavioral manipulation, unlawful discrimination, and constitutional rights infringement. Most private sector impact assessment obligations were removed in final version.
  • AI systems must not engage in behavioral manipulation or unlawful discrimination. Prohibition on AI systems designed to produce CSAM or non-consensual deepfakes.
California TFAIA
United States — California
High
Effective: September 29 2025
  • Requires frontier AI developers to publish safety and security protocols and conduct third-party audits. Targets large developers (>$5M compute on a single model, >$100M aggregate).
  • Large AI model developers must implement written safety protocols, publish them, and submit to independent audits. Enforcement by California Division of Homeland Security.
California ADMT
United States — California
High
Effective: January 1 2026 (risk assessments required)
  • Consumers have right to opt out of ADMT in housing, employment, credit, or healthcare decisions. Businesses must conduct privacy risk assessments for significant ADMT use.
  • AI systems making or substantially influencing significant decisions must offer opt-out, conduct risk assessments, and provide transparency. Applies to any business subject to CCPA.
Connecticut AI Safety Act
United States — Connecticut
High
Effective: May 27 2026
  • Multi-part framework covering safety, transparency, and consumer protection for AI systems. Part of the accelerating US state-level AI governance patchwork.
  • AI systems must meet transparency and safety standards. Joins Texas TRAIGA and California regulations in creating a de facto multi-state compliance floor for US AI deployments.
South Korea AI Framework Act
South Korea
High
Effective: January 2026
  • First comprehensive AI law in Asia-Pacific. Risk-based approach aligned with OECD AI Principles. Establishes AI safety standards, transparency requirements, and a national AI governance structure.
  • High-impact AI systems require risk assessment and transparency disclosures. Significant for organizations with South Korean operations or customers.
Saudi Arabia PDPL + AI Framework
Saudi Arabia
High
Effective: PDPL: September 14 2024; AI Adoption Framework: 2026 (public sector mandatory)
  • Saudi declared 2026 the Year of AI. Mandatory AI Adoption Framework for public sector with five pillars: data governance, model accountability, transparency, human oversight, risk management. PDPL full compliance January 1 2027.
  • AI systems processing Saudi personal data must comply with PDPL. Public sector AI deployments must implement the five-pillar AI Adoption Framework. SDAIA has issued 48 violation decisions in 2024-2025 — enforcement is real.
